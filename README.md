# India cyber fraud: official resources

A curated list of **official** Indian resources on cyber fraud, financial cybercrime, reporting portals and the main statutes. It is meant for the public, journalists, students, compliance staff and lawyers who need reliable starting points — not a complete legal encyclopaedia.

Maintained by deskworkslabs. Last checked: **13 November 2026** (Asia/Calcutta).

If a link here stops working, please open an issue. Unverified or unofficial links are not added.

> **If you have just lost money or think a fraud is under way:** call the National Cybercrime Helpline **1930**, report at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/), and contact your bank at once. Do not pay a further sum, share an OTP or install a remote-access app because someone on the phone told you to.

## If you need to act now

1. **Financial cyber fraud:** call **1930**. The Indian Cyber Crime Coordination Centre (I4C), Ministry of Home Affairs, states that the National Helpline 1930 runs in all States and Union Territories, and that complaints can also be made on the National Cybercrime Reporting Portal. See [I4C — NCRP](https://i4c.mha.gov.in/ncrp.aspx) and the [portal FAQ](https://cybercrime.gov.in/Webform/FAQ.aspx).
2. **File or complete a complaint** on the [National Cybercrime Reporting Portal](https://cybercrime.gov.in/). Official FAQ material on the Citizen Financial Cyber Fraud Reporting system says that after a 1930 call, a formal complaint should be registered on the portal (using the acknowledgement sent by SMS) — do this promptly; do not wait.
3. **Tell your bank or payment app** immediately and ask for the unauthorised transaction to be blocked or frozen. Keep the UTR / UPI / card reference.
4. **Emergency or a threat to safety:** call **112**. The portal FAQ also lists the national women helpline **181**.
5. **Suspected fraud calls, SMS or WhatsApp (you have not lost money yet):** report them on [Sanchar Saathi — Chakshu](https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/sfc/). Chakshu is not a substitute for 1930 or the cybercrime portal if money has already been lost.

## Official reporting portals

- [National Cybercrime Reporting Portal](https://cybercrime.gov.in/) — file and track cybercrime complaints. Initiative of the Government of India, Ministry of Home Affairs / I4C.
- [Portal FAQ](https://cybercrime.gov.in/Webform/FAQ.aspx) and [helpline page](https://cybercrime.gov.in/Webform/Helpline.aspx)
- [Suspect repository search](https://cybercrime.gov.in/Webform/suspect_search_repository.aspx) — the portal lets citizens search certain identifiers against I4C’s repository. This is a look-up facility, not a finding of guilt.
- [Sanchar Saathi](https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/) — Department of Telecommunications citizen portal (also at [www.sancharsaathi.gov.in](https://www.sancharsaathi.gov.in/)). Services include checking mobile connections taken in your name, reporting a lost or stolen handset, and related telecom-security tools.
- [Chakshu — report suspected fraud communication](https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/sfc/) — report suspected fraud calls, SMS or WhatsApp (for example impersonation as a government official, bank or KYC message, investment pitch). Official note: if you have already lost money, use **1930** or [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/).
- [CEIR](https://www.ceir.gov.in/) — Central Equipment Identity Register, for blocking a lost or stolen mobile handset (also reachable through Sanchar Saathi).
- [RBI Complaint Management System](https://cms.rbi.org.in/) — complaints against RBI-regulated entities, including banks, after you have first approached the entity concerned.
- [I4C — National Cybercrime Reporting Portal (explainer)](https://i4c.mha.gov.in/ncrp.aspx)

## Laws and official texts

Use the official text, not a summary. The three new criminal laws came into force on 1 July 2024 (as recorded on India Code). Correspondence tables and official PDFs are published by NCRB on the Ministry of Home Affairs domain.

- [NCRB / MHA “New Criminal Laws” portal](https://www.ncrb.mha.gov.in/uploads/SankalanPortal/Index.html) — official texts and corresponding-section tables
  - [Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (official PDF)](https://www.ncrb.mha.gov.in/uploads/SankalanPortal/DownloadPDF/BNS2023.pdf)
  - [Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (official PDF)](https://www.ncrb.mha.gov.in/uploads/SankalanPortal/DownloadPDF/BNSS2023.pdf)
  - [Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (official PDF)](https://www.ncrb.mha.gov.in/uploads/SankalanPortal/DownloadPDF/BSA2023.pdf)
  - Correspondence tables: [BNS with IPC](https://www.ncrb.mha.gov.in/uploads/SankalanPortal/SectionTableBNS.html) · [BNSS with CrPC](https://www.ncrb.mha.gov.in/uploads/SankalanPortal/SectionTableBNSS.html) · [BSA with the Indian Evidence Act](https://www.ncrb.mha.gov.in/uploads/SankalanPortal/SectionTableBSA.html)
- [India Code](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/) — official Central Acts, including the Information Technology Act, 2000 and the Prevention of Money-Laundering Act, 2002
- [MeitY — Information Technology Act, 2000](https://www.meity.gov.in/content/information-technology-act-2000)

The Information Technology Act, 2000 remains in force alongside BNS/BNSS/BSA. Commonly used provisions in cyber-fraud matters include sections 43, 65, 66, 66B, 66C, 66D, 66E, 66F, 67, 67A, 67B, 72 and 72A. Section 66A was struck down by the Supreme Court in *Shreya Singhal v. Union of India* (2015) and should not be invoked.

## Advisories and investor cautions

- [CERT-In published vulnerability notes](https://www.cert-in.org.in/s2cMainServlet?pageid=PUBVLNOTES02)
- [RBI press releases](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_PressReleaseDisplay.aspx)
- [SEBI press releases / media](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebiweb/home/HomeAction.do?doListing=yes&sid=7&ssid=1&smid=0)
- SEBI, *Caution to Investors against Stock Tips offered through Invitation-only WhatsApp Groups*, 22 October 2026: [official PDF](https://www.sebi.gov.in/sebi_data/attachdocs/oct-2026/1792675550417.pdf). Investors are advised not to join such groups (especially those run from overseas numbers), not to rely on planted profit messages, to treat a “the old group was banned, join this new one” message as a red flag, and to deal only with SEBI-registered intermediaries.
- [Sanchar Saathi — Chakshu](https://sancharsaathi.gov.in/sfc/) for reporting suspected fraud communications
